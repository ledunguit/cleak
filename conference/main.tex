\begin{abstract}
Memory leaks (CWE-401) in C/C++ are hard to find: static analysis is
noisy; dynamic analysis covers only exercised paths. We present
\textbf{CLeak}: \emph{the LLM owns policy, the engine
owns mechanism}. The LLM discovers and freezes project-specific
knowledge into a cached profile, judging only
borderline verdicts; a two-tier protocol separates deterministic from
LLM-assisted variance. On the validated Juliet corpus
(1{,}658 cases), an ablation isolates a deterministic
static+dynamic+planner configuration at Precision~96.5\%,
Recall~78.0\%, F1~0.863, MCC~0.790; unconstrained LLM tool selection
costs 4$\times$ more for \emph{lower} accuracy (F1 0.991 on
\texttt{malloc}, 0.584 on \texttt{strdup} sites). On LAMeD it holds
100\% precision at 30.0\% recall, and 100\% at 46.2\% on a
reconstructed MemHint-project corpus. Caveats are stated
wherever evidence is incomplete.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The missing release of memory after its effective lifetime, catalogued
as CWE-401, is a longstanding defect class in C and C++, where manual
memory management remains the norm in kernels, browsers, databases, and
firmware. Unlike a crash-inducing bug (a buffer overflow or
use-after-free), a leak rarely terminates its host process: it surfaces
as slow resource exhaustion, degraded availability, or denial of
service, hard to reproduce and costly to diagnose in production.
Finding leaks has meant two incompatible tool families.
\textbf{Static analyzers} (Clang Static Analyzer, Infer, CodeQL) reason
about all paths without running the program: coverage plus a
structurally high false-positive rate, since they cannot tell a genuine
leak from an allocation whose ownership legitimately moved to a caller.
\textbf{Dynamic analyzers} (Valgrind Memcheck, AddressSanitizer,
LeakSanitizer) instrument and run the actual binary: near-zero false
positives on exercised paths, zero visibility into the often much larger
unexercised set. Neither family suffices, and concatenating their
findings does not resolve their disagreement.

Large language models offer a third mode: reading a function's source
and calling context and reasoning about ownership like a human reviewer,
including project-specific conventions no generic tool knows (a wrapper
like \texttt{cJSON\_Duplicate} is an allocator; a parameter freed on the
success path but not an error path leaks there). The naive alternative,
an LLM agent with unrestricted tool access that decides what to
investigate, brings what a controlled scientific evaluation
cannot tolerate: run-to-run non-determinism, even at temperature~0, from
provider-side batching and scheduling, plus unconstrained, expensive
tool calls that are hard to audit or bound in cost.

CLeak's contribution is an architecture that captures an LLM's
diagnostic value without its instability, built on the principle of
Section~\ref{sec:policy-mechanism}: \emph{the LLM owns policy, the
engine owns mechanism}. This paper makes four contributions;
Section~\ref{sec:contributions} returns to them with measured results.

\begin{description}
\item[C1: A deterministic-except-judge hybrid pipeline.] A pinned
  build-and-sanitize recipe runs LeakSanitizer without LLM involvement
  whenever the build command is known, keeping dynamic evidence
  deterministic. With deterministic static evidence and
  LLM-planner-gated orchestration, it is the system's \emph{strongest
  measured result} on the full validated Juliet corpus (F1~0.863,
  MCC~0.790; Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}) at a fraction of agentic
  alternatives' cost (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}).
\item[C2: Two-tier reproducibility protocol.] A deterministic
  (\texttt{no\_llm}) configuration proven bitwise-reproducible by a
  dedicated determinism gate, and an LLM-assisted (\texttt{llm\_assisted})
  one whose variance is measured and reported rather than hidden
  (Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}).
\item[C3: Structured evidence enrichment.] Ownership-transfer tracking,
  alloc$\to$free pairing with guard-subset path reconciliation (a free
  reconciles an allocation only if its guard conditions are a subset of
  the exit path's), path-sensitive and parameter-ownership leak detection
  (a leak on only some control-flow paths, or only when a parameter is
  freed on some branches), and
  runtime$\leftrightarrow$candidate correlation, which together make the
  deterministic heuristic judge path-aware without an SMT solver
  (Section~\ref{sec:judging}).
\item[C4: A self-consistency consensus judge, evaluated against single-LLM
  judging.] A judging layer that fuses static and dynamic evidence and,
  only for genuinely borderline bundles (heuristic verdict
  \texttt{likely\_leak} or \texttt{uncertain}, or confidence in
  $[0.35, 0.7]$), resamples the LLM judge $K$ times at nonzero
  temperature and combines votes by self-consistency. On a
  family-stratified sample spanning the corpus's difficulty spread,
  single-LLM judging is both more stable (2.0\% vs.\ 8.0\% verdict
  flips) and more accurate (F1 0.852 to 0.855 vs.\ 0.793 to 0.796) than
  $K{=}3$ consensus. We do not recommend consensus judging by default
  (Section~\ref{sec:eval-consensus}).
\end{description}

We evaluate CLeak on the NIST Juliet CWE-401 suite (a validated,
integrity-checked 1{,}658-case subset), the peer-reviewed LAMeD
benchmark (41 developer-confirmed leaks, seven C projects), an
independently reconstructed MemHint-project corpus (19 leak-fix commits
across six MemHint target projects, since its own per-bug list is
unpublished), and a live Clang Static Analyzer re-run under identical
scoring. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} reports a controlled five-factor
ablation over the \emph{entire} validated corpus (not a sub-sample), by
allocator family, alongside results we report as inconclusive or
partially superseded rather than omit.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Static analysis for memory leaks.}
Clang Static Analyzer~\cite{clangsa} performs path-sensitive symbolic
execution, Infer~\cite{infer} scales via compositional bi-abduction,
and CodeQL~\cite{codeql} expresses leak patterns as database queries;
all three inherit the same limitation: without execution, ownership
transfer and conditional deallocation stay ambiguous, so false
positives rise. Tree-sitter~\cite{treesitter} is the incremental-parsing
substrate below; NESA~\cite{nesa} and scaling symbolic
execution~\cite{symexec-scaling} point toward combining learned and
symbolic reasoning, a direction CLeak takes with an LLM. The engine
itself follows architectural precedent: content-hash-keyed parse
memoization (Section~\ref{sec:arch}) mirrors Infer's compositional
summaries~\cite{infer-verification}, its stateless, independently
callable MCP services mirror Google's shardable
Tricorder~\cite{tricorder}, and CodeQL's incremental
reuse~\cite{codeql-incremental} motivates the dynamic analyzer's
compiler-cache-backed rebuilds.

\paragraph{Dynamic analysis for memory leaks.}
Valgrind Memcheck~\cite{valgrind} and
AddressSanitizer / LeakSanitizer~\cite{asan} run an instrumented
binary: near-perfect precision on exercised paths at the cost of
coverage; recent sanitizer work (RangeSanitizer~\cite{rangesan},
QMSan~\cite{qmsan}, CombiSan~\cite{combisan}, AirTaint~\cite{airtaint})
improves speed and precision for related memory-safety classes but not
the coverage ceiling. Fuzzers' true-positive sets are largely disjoint
from static analyzers'~\cite{fuzzer-vs-sa}, motivating a hybrid rather
than a choice between them (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline});
AddressWatcher~\cite{addresswatcher} localizes leak \emph{fixes}, a
complementary problem.

\paragraph{LLMs for software defect detection.}
Early empirical work found LLMs can identify vulnerabilities from
source alone with bounded accuracy~\cite{llm-vuln-eval};
IRIS~\cite{iris} couples an LLM with static analysis; prompting
alone can \emph{exceed} a static analyzer on its
benchmark~\cite{llm-proxy-static}, a finding compatible with CLeak,
which grounds the LLM in tool output;
VulnLLM-R~\cite{vulnllmr} and SemTaint~\cite{semtaint} address broader
vulnerability classes. The closest prior work targets C/C++ memory
leaks directly: \textbf{MemHint}~\cite{memhint}, a neuro-symbolic
static pipeline reporting 52 to 54 leaks found (49 confirmed/fixed)
across 7 projects (3.4M+ LOC), and \textbf{LAMeD}~\cite{lamed}, whose
LLM only \emph{annotates} source for a classical analyzer: not agentic,
no F1 reported, and the source of our real-world benchmark corpus
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-lamed}); a related line targets kernel-level
memory-management intention inconsistencies~\cite{kernel-memmgmt}.
Repository-scale agentic auditors (RepoAudit~\cite{repoaudit},
Revelio~\cite{revelio}, SAILOR~\cite{sailor}) and crash-oriented
systems (FuzzingBrain~V2~\cite{fuzzingbrainv2},
ATLANTIS~\cite{atlantis}) are our architectural analogues but target
multi-defect classes; RepoAudit's reported P=78.4\% spans several
defect types, so we do not treat it as a leak-specific baseline.
Agent-design foundations (ReAct~\cite{react},
Reflexion~\cite{reflexion}, Tree-of-Thoughts~\cite{tot},
SWE-agent~\cite{sweagent}, Agentless~\cite{agentless}) shape our
Stage~A/B sub-agent mechanisms (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}).

\paragraph{Reducing LLM variance and LLM-as-judge.}
Self-consistency decoding~\cite{selfconsistency} samples reasoning
chains at nonzero temperature and votes; our consensus judge (C4)
applies it to leak verdicts, not chain-of-thought answers.
Zheng et al.'s LLM-as-a-judge calibration study~\cite{llmjudge}
motivates reporting McNemar-test significance
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-consensus}) rather than a raw accuracy delta.

\paragraph{Benchmarks and protocols.}
Beyond our evaluation corpora Juliet~\cite{juliet} and
LAMeD~\cite{lamed-benchmark}, DiverseVul~\cite{diversevul},
SV-COMP Memsafety~\cite{svcomp}, and Magma~\cite{magma} are benchmarks
we position against but do not use; the Model Context
Protocol~\cite{mcp} connects our orchestrator to the analyzers
(Section~\ref{sec:arch}), and a pointer-ownership model for
C~\cite{ownership-model} informs our judge's ownership-transfer
vocabulary.

\paragraph{Positioning.} To our knowledge, no 2025-to-2026 system
combines static and dynamic evidence for \emph{non-crash} C/C++ memory
leaks with agentic orchestration and a reproducibility protocol
separating deterministic from LLM-dependent results; CLeak targets this
gap, and of these systems only it exposes an MCP tool transport and
generates a source-anchored repair diff. POM~\cite{pom} (an LLM+SAT
pointer-ownership enforcement tool) and SecVulEval~\cite{secvuleval} (a
C/C++ vulnerability dataset, not a detector) complete the contrast.

\section{System Architecture and Investigation Pipeline}
\label{sec:arch}

\subsection{Components and policy}
\label{sec:policy-mechanism}

CLeak is a standalone CLI/TUI scanner, \texttt{leak-inspector-tui}, the
sole orchestrator: it drives two analyzer services and an LLM provider
over the Model Context Protocol (MCP) on Streamable-HTTP
(JSON-RPC~2.0), with no shared database and no server-side state beyond
per-run artifacts. It is the sole MCP client of the static analyzer
(port 50061) and the dynamic analyzer (port 50062) and the sole HTTP
client of the LLM gateway; the analyzers never communicate with each
other or with the LLM. All findings
converge on one structure: a \texttt{LeakCandidate} (an allocation
site) accumulates \texttt{Evidence} entries into a \texttt{LeakBundle}
that the judging layer (Section~\ref{sec:judging}) resolves into a
\texttt{VerdictResult} written by code, never by free-form LLM text;
run artifacts (reports, a cross-run-comparable \texttt{snapshot.json},
an event log) go to \texttt{results/\textlangle scanId\textrangle/}.

The architecture follows one principle: \emph{the LLM owns
policy, the engine owns mechanism}. Per-project variation (which
functions allocate memory, ownership conventions, dynamic-analysis
aggressiveness, verdict-threshold calibration) is discovered once by an
LLM, exported as a structured profile, \emph{verified} against its
source (a grep-based anti-hallucination check), cached, and frozen for
benchmarking; the engine (tree-sitter parsing, control-flow
construction, alloc$\to$free pairing, heuristic scoring, consensus
aggregation) executes deterministically and never depends on the LLM on
the measured evaluation path. Three host-side modules implement the
pattern (Table~\ref{tab:policy}), so onboarding a new project requires
zero lines of new code, and parsed structure is memoized by content
hash rather than recomputed per call
site~\cite{infer-verification}. In the benchmark configuration used
throughout Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}, allocators come from a frozen
manifest and the strategist/tuner are disabled, so \texttt{no\_llm}
makes \emph{zero} non-deterministic LLM calls on the evaluation path,
and its full-corpus Juliet confusion matrix (B3 in
Table~\ref{tab:ablation}: TP1686/FP674/FN892 at $n{=}1{,}658$) is fixed
across runs.

\subsection{MCP tool surface}

The analyzers expose 22 MCP tools; only five reach the Stage~A LLM
sub-agent (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}): \texttt{candidateScan},
\texttt{astScan}, \texttt{functionSummary}, \texttt{pathConstraints},
\texttt{ownershipConventions}. These \emph{content-capable} tools take
inline source content instead of a shared filesystem mount, so both
services stay stateless and independently deployable~\cite{tricorder};
the rest are orchestrator-driven or belong to the deterministic Stage~B
recipe described next.

\subsection{The investigation pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

Static candidates come from a deterministic \texttt{candidateScan} over
allocation sites using the project's frozen allocator profile; each is
bundled and investigated through four stages: static fan-out by LLM
sub-agents and dynamic-evidence gathering run concurrently (Stages A
and B), Stage C synthesizes each bundle with no LLM involvement, and
Stage D, the hybrid judge of Section~\ref{sec:judging}, escalates to
the LLM only for borderline bundles.

\textbf{Stage A: static fan-out.} Candidates are grouped by file
affinity into batches, each assigned to an independent LLM sub-agent
restricted to the five content-capable tools above plus
\texttt{read\_file}. The sub-agent gathers structural evidence
(ownership conventions, path constraints, function-level alloc/free
pairing) but is architecturally unable, by tool partitioning, to record
a verdict; evidence is captured by code wrapping each tool call, not
self-reported by the model, removing one common source of agent
hallucination from the evaluation path.

\textbf{Stage B: dynamic evidence.} When the case carries a known
build command (as Juliet cases do, by NIST's convention), Stage~B runs
a \emph{fixed, deterministic} recipe, \texttt{buildTarget} with
sanitizer flags followed by \texttt{lsanRun}, with no LLM call
whatsoever (part of contribution C1); this is what makes the
\texttt{no\_llm} configuration's dynamic evidence reproducible
bit-for-bit. Only without a known build command does an LLM dynamic
worker take over: it reads the project's build system and attempts its
own sanitizer build, restricted to the eleven dynamic tools plus
\texttt{read\_file}, running concurrently with Stage~A.

\textbf{Stage C: synthesis.} A deterministic merge stamps each bundle
with a \texttt{dynamicCoverage} state (\texttt{exercised\_clean},
\texttt{exercised\_leak}, \texttt{not\_exercised},
\texttt{dynamic\_off}) that the judging layer treats as ground truth
about what was observed at runtime; no LLM call occurs here.

\textbf{Stage D: hybrid judging} is described in
Section~\ref{sec:judging}. Any model-call or parse failure at any stage
falls back to the deterministic heuristic verdict, so the system is
architecturally biased toward being no worse than its deterministic core,
never toward silent failure.

\section{Judging Layer}
\label{sec:judging}

CLeak's judging layer has three interchangeable configurations,
evaluated head-to-head throughout Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}: a
purely \textbf{heuristic judge}, a \textbf{single-LLM judge}, and a
\textbf{consensus judge}. The heuristic judge runs first and scores
every bundle; only \emph{borderline} bundles are escalated.

\subsection{Heuristic judge: deterministic, path-sensitive}

The heuristic judge (contribution C3) sums a fixed table of weighted
evidence signals per bundle: correlated runtime leaks (weighted by
sanitizer leak kind: \texttt{definitely\_lost}/\texttt{asan\_leak} weigh
most, \texttt{possibly\_lost} least), structural missing-free patterns,
unpaired or conditionally-freed allocations, \emph{path-sensitive} and
\emph{parameter-ownership} leak signals, and an
ownership-transfer penalty when the allocation is legitimately handed to a
caller. It bands the total against fixed thresholds,
$\mathit{confirmed}=0.7$ and $\mathit{likely}=0.4$, frozen for every
evaluation run reported in this paper. Three precision gates can override
the additive score outright: a callee-freed allocation with no correlated
runtime leak is forced to \texttt{likely\_false\_positive}; a clean
dynamic run with no decisive static evidence exculpates a candidate; and
any ``flagged'' verdict lacking at least one \emph{strong} signal (a
correlated runtime leak, a high-confidence structural pattern, an unpaired
allocation, a path-sensitive leak, or an ownership issue) is downgraded to
\texttt{uncertain} rather than reported as a leak.

\textbf{Path sensitivity without an SMT solver.} CLeak computes path
feasibility with a lightweight heuristic rather than an SMT solver, so
guard analysis is available on every function with no solver-timeout or
memory-ceiling failure mode. The mechanism is \emph{guard-subset
reconciliation}: a free on one control-flow path
reconciles an allocation only if that free's guard condition set is a
\emph{subset} of the exit path's guard set; a free reachable under
a strictly narrower condition than the corresponding leak exit does not
count as covering it. This CFG heuristic, combined with
conservative dead-code reachability (dropping exit paths that follow an
unconditional terminator in the same block), lets even the
deterministic \texttt{no\_llm} judge catch a pointer freed on
a function's success path but lost on its error path (a
\emph{path-sensitive leak}), or a parameter freed on some branches but not
others (a \emph{parameter-ownership leak}): the exact pattern behind
cJSON's \texttt{merge\_patch} case study in
Section~\ref{sec:eval-lamed}.

\subsection{LLM judge: rubric-based, borderline-only}

A bundle escalates to the LLM judge when the heuristic verdict is
\emph{borderline} (\texttt{likely\_leak} or \texttt{uncertain}, or
confidence in $[0.35, 0.7]$) \emph{or} contradicts dynamic evidence
(e.g.\ flagged as a leak while a correlated sanitizer run came back
clean). The LLM sees the candidate's enclosing function source (labels
stripped from comments to avoid leaking benchmark ground truth), a
static-evidence summary, dynamic findings tagged by correlation
strength (\texttt{LINKED} / same-file-different-site / \texttt{CLEAN}),
and, when available, the project's LLM-discovered ownership
conventions. It returns one of five labels with a confidence and an
explanation under an explicit priority rubric: a linked runtime leak is
decisive; ownership transfer is decisive for false positives; the
heuristic judge's path-sensitive and parameter-ownership rules apply;
\texttt{uncertain} is reserved for genuinely insufficient evidence,
never a default. Output is constrained to JSON; any parse failure
silently falls back to the heuristic verdict.

\subsection{Consensus judge: self-consistency voting (C4)}

When enabled ($K>1$), the same LLM-judge call is resampled $K$ times at a
nonzero consensus temperature and merged by one of three rules:
majority, a confidence-weighted vote (dynamic-contradicting votes
down-weighted $\times 0.3$), or unanimous-to-flag. A veto rule
adds asymmetry by design: a strong heuristic exculpation (a
\texttt{false\_positive}/\texttt{likely\_false\_positive} verdict at
confidence $\geq 0.75$) can only \emph{remove} a flag from the consensus
outcome, never add one. We do not assume voting
over $K$ samples reduces case-level instability in practice:
Section~\ref{sec:eval-consensus} evaluates it directly and reports a
sample-composition-dependent finding, not a clean win.

\section{Reproducibility Protocol}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

An LLM-in-the-loop system cannot honestly claim bit-identical
reproducibility, and we do not: CLeak instead reports a
\textbf{two-tier} protocol (contribution C2) that distinguishes what is
and is not deterministic.

\textbf{Tier 1: \texttt{no\_llm}, bitwise-deterministic.} With the
strategist/tuner disabled and allocators supplied from a frozen manifest,
the heuristic judge, the deterministic Stage~B recipe, and the scoring
pipeline produce byte-identical output across runs. A dedicated
determinism gate enforces this and explicitly rejects the two
\emph{false-pass} modes it caught during development: a timestamp
collision that made the tool compare a run against itself, and a
degenerate run in which an unreachable analyzer made every case error out
\emph{identically}, registering as spurious determinism.

\textbf{Tier 2: \texttt{llm\_assisted}, reported variance.} Because
even temperature-0 LLM calls are not bit-identical across runs, we
run each \texttt{llm\_assisted} configuration $N$ times and report
mean$\pm$std for every metric, plus a \emph{case-level
verdict-stability} analysis. We surface a
\textbf{stable-by-luck} phenomenon observed directly: two
independent 30-case runs produced an \emph{identical} aggregate confusion
matrix (27~TP/8~FP/5~FN/37~TN) while only 73.3\% of individual
verdicts were stable (a 26.7\% flip rate). Opposite-direction
flips cancelled in the aggregate; reporting only that matrix would have
concealed them, and reporting case-level stability does not.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Experimental setup}

\textbf{Datasets.} Three corpora are used: the validated Juliet
CWE-401 subset (1{,}658 cases across 10 functional families, synthetic,
function-labeled), the peer-reviewed LAMeD benchmark (41 leaks / 50
sites across 7 real C projects, positive-only), and a self-reconstructed
MemHint-derived corpus (19 cases across 6 real C projects,
function-labeled). Juliet passes a five-gate integrity pipeline (schema,
includes, syntax, label-overlap, content-hash) before any evaluation
run; an earlier unvalidated ingest silently dropped 21\% of C++
multi-file class variants from the confusion matrix, so every headline
number is measured on the corrected, validated 1{,}658-case corpus.
LAMeD is positive-only at the function level, so true negatives are
zero by construction; we report recall, precision, and false positives
per KLOC for it, never specificity, accuracy, or MCC.

\textbf{Metrics and scoring.} We report Precision, Recall, F1,
Accuracy, Specificity, and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) by
standard definitions, plus Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and false
positives per thousand non-blank executable lines (FP/KLOC, headers
excluded), one denominator applied uniformly to our results and every
baseline. Scoring is site-based, not finding-based: each ground-truth
site is one sample, so multiple findings in the same leaking function
collapse to one true positive.

\textbf{Baselines.} Clang Static Analyzer is re-run live on the
identical corpus with the identical scorer, a fairness discipline
applied throughout; we also attempted Facebook Infer, but every run
failed its tool-availability check, so no Infer numbers appear. Clang
is itself positive-only (TN$=$0 for its own findings), so
specificity/MCC are dropped from any table including it. MemHint and
LAMeD numbers are quoted from their publications where we do not re-run
them: different corpora and scoring protocols make these comparisons
directional, not a claim of superiority.

\subsection{Full-corpus result}
\label{sec:eval-fullcorpus}

Unlike an earlier family-stratified measurement, all nine capability
configurations below (Section~\ref{sec:eval-ablation}) ran on the
\emph{entire} validated corpus (1{,}658 cases, not a sub-sample), with
static evidence, dynamic evidence, and the LLM judge enabled where each
calls for them. The best end-to-end result is the
deterministic static+dynamic+planner configuration B6a: Precision
96.5\%, Recall 78.0\%, F1~0.863, MCC~0.790 (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), a
substantial improvement over the static-only \texttt{no\_llm} baseline
(F1~0.612, MCC~0.375) it is measured against. Because the corpus is full
rather than family-balanced, per-family performance is not uniform; we
report the full distribution, not just the aggregate.

\subsection{Capability ablation: five orchestration factors}
\label{sec:eval-ablation}

To isolate \emph{which} architectural factor drives performance, we ran
nine declarative baseline configurations (B1 to B7, with two sub-variants
of B6) sweeping five independent factors (static evidence, dynamic
evidence, an LLM planner, agentic tool-selection, and evidence fusion) on
the full 1{,}658-case Juliet corpus; each LLM-assisted configuration (B4
to B7) was repeated three times for mean $\pm$ std.
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} gives full confusion matrices and real dollar
cost at DeepSeek's published per-token rate.

Three findings hold at full-corpus scale. First, dynamic evidence is the
dominant false-positive suppressor: adding it to the
LLM-judge-plus-static configuration (B4$\to$B6) drops false positives
from 470 to 74 (an $\sim$85\% reduction) while raising F1 from 0.801 to
0.862. Second, the static-evidence tools \texttt{functionSummary} and
\texttt{pathConstraints} are synergistic rather than additive: a
separate $n{=}50$ stratified ablation shows that enabling either alone
leaves the confusion matrix identical to candidate-scan-only, and only
both together lift recall 0.792$\to$0.943 ($+8$ true positives). Third,
unconstrained agentic tool selection is counter-productive at
full-corpus scale: B6a (6{,}155 tokens/case, \$6.63 total) beats
B6b/B7's agentic loop ($\sim$31K tokens/case, $\sim$4$\times$ the
dollar cost each) on F1 (0.863 vs.\ 0.858/0.856) and on false-positive
density (FP/KLOC 0.133 vs.\ 0.192/0.186). The LLM planner chooses well
when constrained to \emph{when} to run static or dynamic analysis;
given latitude over \emph{which} tool to call next, it adds cost
without benefit.

The B6a configuration is not model-specific: re-running the full
1{,}658-case B6a configuration end-to-end on two further providers,
mimo-v2.5 (F1~0.737, P~0.880 / R~0.633, MCC~0.611, \$2.11) and GLM-5.2
(F1~0.774, P~0.763 / R~0.786; single complete run), keeps the advantage
over the static-only baseline (F1~0.612) intact, though absolute numbers
track each model's reasoning profile. These runs use the same frozen
corpus hash and scorer as Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.

Performance is not uniform across allocator families: family-level F1
runs from 0.991 (\texttt{malloc}-family sites, realloc-augmented
allocations, $n{=}294$) to 0.584 (\texttt{strdup}-family, $n{=}348$
sites across 96 test cases), the latter driven by low recall (41.3\%)
rather than false positives (precision remains 100\%), with the
intermediate families between 0.85 and 0.89. One representative miss
(\texttt{CWE401\_Memory\_Leak\_\_strdup\_char\_12}, flow-variant 12)
pinpoints a concrete mechanism: the case's true leak
sits behind two \emph{independent} randomized branches (a heap allocation
via \texttt{strdup} vs.\ a stack allocation via \texttt{alloca}, and
whether \texttt{free} executes), so a single dynamic run exercises only
one random path. When that path is non-leaking, the heuristic judge
combines the resulting \texttt{exercised\_clean} dynamic signal with a
coarse, not fully guard-reconciled, static alloc/free pairing into a
confident (0.8) false-positive verdict without escalating to the LLM
judge. A structurally identical \texttt{malloc}-family case did
escalate and was judged correctly, so the mechanism is not specific to
\texttt{strdup} as an allocator. We regard it as one verified contributor
to the family's low recall, not an exhaustive account of all 59 affected
cases; the two smallest families (\texttt{destructor},
\texttt{virtual}, $n{=}3$ and $n{=}5$ cases respectively) are too small
here for their own F1 to be statistically meaningful.

A separate small-sample $2\times2$ ablation crossing LLM orchestration
against dynamic evidence, on a subset of Juliet cases, agreed with these
findings.

\subsection{Consensus judge: stability and significance}
\label{sec:eval-consensus}

On a family-stratified $n{=}50$ sample, across two independent
campaigns, single-LLM judging ($K{=}1$) is both the more stable arm
(verdict-flip rate 2.0\% vs.\ consensus $K{=}3$'s 8.0\%) and the more
accurate one (F1 0.852 to 0.855 across its two campaigns vs.\
consensus's 0.793 to 0.796); a paired McNemar test (campaign A, 205
sites) agrees (single-LLM 91.7\%/F1~0.852 vs.\ consensus
89.0\%/F1~0.793, 7 of 8 discordant sites favoring single-LLM,
$\chi^2 = 3.13$, $p = 0.077$, not significant at this sample size). We
therefore do not recommend enabling consensus judging by default; it
remains available as an opt-in configuration
(\texttt{consensus.n~>~1}) pending a larger, multi-seed study.

\subsection{Real-world validation: LAMeD and MemHint-derived}
\label{sec:eval-lamed}

On the 41-case, 7-project LAMeD benchmark (50 scoreable sites; dynamic
analysis is not exercised on these static-only configurations), the
deterministic \texttt{no\_llm} configuration holds 100\% precision
(TP~15/FP~0/FN~35) at 30.0\% recall in every configuration we measured,
with \texttt{llm\_assisted} reproducing the identical matrix with zero
variance across 3 independent runs. A manual taxonomy of the six
confirmed cJSON leaks finds four structurally distinct difficulty
classes, consistent with LAMeD's own finding that even
state-of-the-art tools catch only 5 to 10 of 43 real leaks.
\texttt{interproceduralFlow} adds no measurable value: its alloc/free
counting is not path-sensitive, so on cJSON's \texttt{merge\_patch}
leak it counts two mutually exclusive \texttt{cJSON\_Delete(target)}
frees as covering the allocation and misses the never-freed error path,
leaving the \texttt{+interproceduralFlow} configuration byte-identical
to the default across all 41 cases. MemHint~\cite{memhint} publishes no
per-bug list, so we evaluate on an \emph{independently reconstructed}
corpus of 19 cases across 6 of MemHint's target projects (26 sites,
positive-only): \texttt{no\_llm} reaches 100\% precision (12 of 26
sites) at 46.2\% recall, three \texttt{llm\_assisted} runs are identical
to it and to each other (P~1.000$\pm$0.000, R~0.462$\pm$0.000,
F1~0.632$\pm$0.000), and the comparison to MemHint's own headline
numbers (52 to 54 leaks across 7 projects,
Table~\ref{tab:literature}) remains directional, the denominators
differing.

false-positive density. Table~\ref{tab:literature} places our results
alongside literature numbers for MemHint and LAMeD; different corpora
and scoring protocols keep that comparison directional rather than
conclusive.

\subsection{Allocator-profiler validation}

Because the allocator profiler (Table~\ref{tab:policy}) is itself an LLM
call, we validate it against a frozen hand-written
allocator/deallocator list for cJSON: 92\% allocator recall and 100\%
deallocator recall, at 35\%/67\% precision, with most apparent false
positives being allocators the hand-written list had missed
(e.g.\ \texttt{cJSON\_Parse}, \texttt{cJSON\_Print}). Aggregated over
all seven LAMeD projects, discovery quality drops substantially
(allocator F1 0.22, deallocator F1 0.17): the protocol validates
per-project allocator knowledge only where a project exposes a small,
conventional allocation API, and does not generalize to every real
project.

\paragraph{Contribution recap.}
\label{sec:contributions}

C1 pins the dynamic stage to a fixed build-and-sanitize recipe and gates
the orchestration behind an LLM planner, the system's strongest measured
configuration on the full validated Juliet corpus: Precision 96.5\%,
Recall 78.0\%, F1 0.863, MCC 0.790, against F1 0.612 for the
static-only baseline, at about \$6.63 versus \$26 to \$27 for the
agentic alternatives (B6b/B7). C2 proves the \texttt{no\_llm}
configuration bitwise-reproducible with a dedicated determinism gate
that rejects the two false-pass modes observed during development, and
reports the \texttt{llm\_assisted} configuration's variance rather than
hiding it. C3's guard-subset reconciliation of alloc$\to$free pairing
lets the purely deterministic heuristic judge detect path-sensitive and
parameter-ownership leaks without an SMT solver. C4 finds single-LLM
judging more stable (verdict flip rate 2.0\% vs.\ 8.0\%) and more
accurate (F1 0.852 to 0.855 vs.\ 0.793 to 0.796) than $K{=}3$
consensus, so we do not recommend enabling consensus judging by
default.

\section{Discussion, Open Problems, and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Open Problems}
\label{sec:openproblems}

Three issues remain open, each analyzed where it is measured; we
restate them as the standing agenda.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\texttt{strdup}-family recall; smallest families.} The
  weakest Juliet family for B6a (F1 0.584, recall-limited at 100\%
  precision), where the verified two-branch dynamic-coverage mechanism
  does not account for all 59 affected cases; \texttt{destructor} and
  \texttt{virtual} ($n{=}3$ and $n{=}5$ cases) are reported for
  completeness only, too small for statistically meaningful F1.
\item \textbf{\texttt{interproceduralFlow} adds no measured recall;
  allocator-profiler discovery does not generalize.} Alloc/free
  counting is not path-sensitive: on cJSON's \texttt{merge\_patch} leak
  it counts two mutually exclusive \texttt{cJSON\_Delete(target)} frees
  as covering the allocation and misses the never-freed error path,
  leaving \texttt{+interproceduralFlow} byte-identical to the default on
  all 41 LAMeD cases; and aggregated over the seven LAMeD projects,
  allocator and deallocator discovery drops to F1 0.22 and 0.17,
  validating per-project allocator knowledge only where a project
  exposes a small, conventional API.
\item \textbf{Consensus judging is not recommended by default; the
  agentic cost finding is corpus-specific.} Single-LLM judging was more
  stable and more accurate than $K{=}3$ consensus on the
  family-stratified sample, so consensus stays opt-in pending a larger,
  multi-seed study; and Section~\ref{sec:eval-ablation} shows agentic
  tool selection costs real money for no accuracy gain on Juliet, whose
  leak patterns are largely intraprocedural and well served by a fixed
  recipe: at full-corpus scale, B6b/B7 spend $\sim$5$\times$ the tokens
  of B6a but $\sim$4$\times$ the dollar cost (\$26 to \$27 vs.\ \$6.63),
  while more diverse corpora should shift the balance toward agentic
  selection.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Discussion and Threats to Validity}

\textbf{Per-family variation, path-sensitivity without SMT,
architectural precedent, and judge non-determinism.} B6a's full-corpus
F1 (0.863) is not uniform across Juliet's ten functional families:
\texttt{malloc} is nearly solved (F1~0.991),
\texttt{strdup} lags (F1~0.584, recall-limited), a gap traced to a
verified mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:eval-ablation}); every ablation in
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} agrees dynamic evidence is the most
effective lever against false positives, more than any LLM-side
intervention, so future iterations should prioritize dynamic-analysis
coverage (e.g.\ the opt-in targeted-harness stage of
Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}, not evaluated here) over LLM-judge
sophistication. Guard-subset reconciliation (Section~\ref{sec:judging})
reasons about guard containment, not numeric constraints, and is
strictly less complete than SMT feasibility, adopted deliberately for
reliability at scale.
Section~\ref{sec:related} aligns CLeak's engine with compositional
analysis~\cite{infer-verification}, shardable stateless
services~\cite{tricorder}, and incremental
reuse~\cite{codeql-incremental,semgrep}. The AST memoization cache is
now disk-persisted and byte-bounded; in one controlled run (a
143-file checkout of LAMeD's \texttt{libxml2} case, \texttt{no\_llm}
mode) a warm repeat scan took 3.0s at 266MiB peak memory versus 54.0s
at 1.03GiB cold ($\sim$18$\times$ less wall clock, $\sim$74\% less
peak memory). Both changes touch neither judge logic nor evidence
content; we expect no precision/recall/F1 number in
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} to change but did not re-run the sweep, an
assumption grounded in the cache's semantics. We do not treat
LLM-judge non-determinism as fixable; the two-tier protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility} exists to measure and report it, not
obscure it behind single-run aggregates, and a self-consistency
technique's apparent benefit depends on whether the sample spans the
corpus's difficulty spread, which is why
Section~\ref{sec:eval-consensus}'s consensus result is
family-stratified.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

We presented CLeak, a memory-leak investigation system for C/C++ built
on one organizing principle: the LLM owns policy, the engine owns
mechanism, so the LLM contributes project-specific reasoning without
inheriting its non-determinism on the measured evaluation path. Our
strongest configuration is a deterministic-except-judge hybrid
pipeline (static evidence, a pinned build-and-sanitize dynamic recipe,
LLM-planner-gated orchestration); a two-tier reproducibility protocol
reports what is and is not reproducible rather than claiming uniform
determinism. Structured, guard-subset-reconciled evidence lets even the
deterministic judge catch path-sensitive and parameter-ownership leaks
without an SMT solver. Evaluated against single-LLM on a
family-stratified sample spanning the corpus's difficulty spread, the
self-consistency consensus judge does not improve verdict stability or
accuracy.

A five-factor ablation on the validated Juliet corpus (1{,}658 cases)
isolates a deterministic static+dynamic+planner configuration at
F1~0.863/MCC~0.790 and roughly one-quarter agentic selection's dollar
cost; on the peer-reviewed LAMeD benchmark it holds 100\% precision at
30.0\% recall under both configurations; on the reconstructed
MemHint-project corpus the pattern recurs at 100\% precision and
46.2\% recall (Section~\ref{sec:eval-lamed}).

Future work has three directions. First, evaluating the opt-in
targeted-harness escalation stage (per-candidate harness synthesis plus
a bounded fuzzing tier)
on the LAMeD and MemHint-derived benchmarks, where whole-project
dynamic evidence is often unavailable. Second, making
\texttt{interproceduralFlow}'s cross-function trace path-sensitive: a
smaller lift than full SMT, it directly targets the
Section~\ref{sec:eval-lamed} gap. Third, bounding the static
evidence stage's cost: on the MemHint-derived corpus the LLM judge was
nearly free ($\sim$17k heuristic verdicts per run, 2 LLM escalations),
but the static sub-agent fan-out spent $\approx$44M tokens across three
runs at $\sim$4.7k MCP calls per case. A gate that escalates to LLM
sub-agents only for candidates the deterministic evidence leaves
ambiguous would make cost scale with genuine ambiguity, not corpus size.
